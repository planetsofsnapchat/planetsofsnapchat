What are Snapchat Planets?

The basics, in plain language.

Snapchat Planets, officially called the Friend Solar System, is a Snapchat+ feature that turns your top eight most-interacted-with friends into planets orbiting around you. You sit at the centre as the sun, and each friend is placed on a ring, from Mercury closest in to Neptune farthest out.

The placement isn't random or a popularity contest set by Snapchat. It's generated from your own activity: how often you snap, chat, and keep streaks with each person. The friend you interact with the most becomes Mercury; the friend who ranks eighth on your list becomes Neptune.

It's a visual, game-like way of showing the same ranking that already powers your Best Friends list, just dressed up as a solar system instead of a plain list of names.

How Snapchat Planets work

Three things decide where a friend lands in your solar system.

Interaction volume

Snaps sent and received, chat messages, and how often you open each other's stories all feed into the ranking behind the scenes.

Streak strength

Long, active streaks tend to push a friend closer to the sun, since they reflect regular, ongoing contact rather than a one-off burst of messages.

Recency

The system leans on recent activity, so a friend you've drifted away from can slide outward toward Neptune even if you were close in the past.

Both people need Snapchat+ and need to switch the feature on for planets to be visible between them. If only one side has it enabled, neither of you will see a planet on the other's profile.

Why Snapchat built a solar system for friendship

The story behind the feature explains a few of its stranger rules.

Friend Solar System launched as a Snapchat+ perk designed to turn an existing, invisible ranking into something people would actually want to open the app and check. Snapchat already calculated a Best Friends list quietly in the background; planets just gave that data a shape, with orbits standing in for closeness and distance standing in for how often two people talk.

The rollout wasn't smooth. Snapchat initially switched the feature on by default for everyone with the subscription, and it didn't take long for people to notice friends drifting outward, or missing from a solar system entirely. For younger users especially, watching a friendship visibly shrink to a distant planet turned out to sting more than a plain list ever had. Snap Inc. responded by pulling the feature back to opt-in: off by default, and only visible between two people who both choose to turn it on.

Opt-in

Off by default on every account; both sides must switch it on before any planet is visible.

Top 8

Only your eight most-interacted-with friends get a planet. Everyone else stays off the map.

Snapchat+

A paid-tier feature; free accounts can't see or generate a solar system at all.

How to turn Snapchat Planets on or off

The feature ships switched off. Here's how to change that on either side.

  1. Confirm you have Snapchat+

    Planets is a Snapchat+ perk. Check your subscription is active under Settings before looking for the toggle.

  2. Open Snapchat+ settings

    From your profile, tap the gear icon, then choose the Snapchat+ section near the top of the settings menu.

  3. Find Manage Features

    Inside Snapchat+ settings, look for "Manage Features" or a similarly labelled list of Snapchat+ extras.

  4. Toggle Friend Solar System

    Switch it on to start showing your planets. Switch it back off at any time; your ranking keeps working in the background, it just stops being shown.

  5. Ask your friend to do the same

    Planets only appear when both people have Snapchat+ and have the feature enabled. One-sided setups won't show anything.

Common issues and fixes

Most planet problems come down to one of these five causes.

No planet shows up on a friend's profile

Check that both accounts have an active Snapchat+ subscription and have switched Friend Solar System on. It only takes one side missing either condition to hide the planet completely.

A planet seems stuck or outdated

Rankings update based on rolling activity, not instantly. Send a few snaps or chats, then close and reopen the app; if it still looks wrong, log out and back in to force a refresh.

A close friend isn't showing as a planet at all

They may not have Snapchat+, or they haven't turned the feature on yet. The setting lives on their account, not yours, so you can't switch it on for them.

The app freezes or crashes when you tap a badge

Update Snapchat to the latest version, clear the app's cache from your phone's settings, and restart the device before trying again.

Your planet order doesn't match how you feel about a friendship

Planets track app activity, not the quality of a friendship. Someone you rarely message on Snapchat but see every day in person can still land near Neptune.

You turned the feature off but a friend can still see an old planet

There can be a short delay before a change takes effect on both sides. Give it a few minutes, and ask the friend to close and reopen the app if the badge still shows.

You can't find the toggle at all

Snapchat rolls out features in stages by region and app version. If Manage Features doesn't list Friend Solar System, update the app fully and check again in a day or two.

What is the Snapchat Best Friends list?

The list that planets are built on top of.

Your Best Friends list is Snapchat's ranking of the people you interact with most, and it's what the planet order is drawn from. It isn't something you choose yourself; the app calculates it from snaps, chats, and streaks, and updates it as your habits change.

Planets simply give that existing list a visual, orbiting shape. If you're curious exactly how the list is built, how to check where you rank with someone, and how the badges tie into it, the dedicated Best Friends page covers all of it.

Using it without letting it get to you

A ranking feature works best treated lightly.

It's easy to read too much into a planet. A friend landing on Neptune instead of Mercury usually just means a busier week, an exam season, or a friendship that lives more in person than in the app. It rarely means the friendship itself has changed.

If checking planets starts to feel stressful rather than fun, turning the feature off costs nothing and can't be seen by anyone else. The underlying Best Friends ranking keeps working quietly in the background either way; only the visible, gamified version goes away.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to the questions people ask most.

Do I need to pay to see Snapchat Planets?

Yes. Friend Solar System is part of Snapchat+, the paid subscription tier, and both people in a friendship need it active to see each other's planets.

Can someone tell that I turned the feature off?

No. Switching Friend Solar System off simply stops planets from being shown; it doesn't send a notification to anyone.

Why is my planet different on two friends' profiles?

Each person has their own solar system based on their own interactions. You might be Mercury to one friend and Saturn to another, depending on how much you interact with each of them individually.

Does unsending a snap change my ranking?

The activity behind the scenes is based on genuine interaction over time, so occasional deletions have little effect on a well-established ranking.
