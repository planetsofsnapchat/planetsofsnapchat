Snapchat Planets, in order

Eight friends, eight planets, one ranking. Here's exactly what Mercury through Neptune mean, how the order is worked out, and what you can do if a placement doesn't feel right.

What decides the order

It isn't random, and it isn't chosen by hand.

Your solar system is generated from the same signals behind your Best Friends list: how many snaps you trade, how often you chat, and how strong your streak is. The eight friends you interact with most become your planets, ranked from Mercury, the closest, to Neptune, the eighth and farthest out.

Because the ranking is based on rolling activity, it isn't permanent. A friend can move from Neptune toward Mercury simply by picking up more frequent contact, and a friend you've gone quiet with can drift the other way.

Every planet, explained

Each entry shows the rank it represents and what it typically signals.

  • 1 MercuryRank #1

    Your single most-interacted-with friend. Mercury usually comes with a long streak and near-daily snaps or chats. This is the person Snapchat's activity data says you're currently closest to.

  • 2 VenusRank #2

    A close second. The gap between Mercury and Venus is often small, and the two can swap places after just a few days of heavier or lighter contact.

  • 3 EarthRank #3

    A frequent, dependable connection. Interaction is regular even if it's slightly less intense than with your top two.

  • 4 MarsRank #4

    Just outside your closest circle. Contact is steady rather than constant, which is enough to keep the friendship solidly inside your top eight.

  • 5 JupiterRank #5

    The midpoint of the list. Jupiter often represents a friendship that matters but doesn't involve daily messaging.

  • 6 SaturnRank #6

    Consistent, if more occasional, contact. You still trade enough snaps and chats to outrank most of your friend list.

  • 7 UranusRank #7

    Lighter, more casual interaction. Uranus friendships are real but tend to involve shorter or less frequent exchanges.

  • 8 NeptuneRank #8

    The edge of your top eight. Still active enough to make the list, but the least frequent contact among the eight, and the first to drop off if a closer friendship pushes in.

What actually moves the needle

A closer look at the signals behind each rank, since "activity" covers a lot of ground.

Snaps over text

Photo and video snaps appear to carry more weight than chat messages alone, since they're the app's original, core interaction.

Two-way beats one-sided

A friend who replies quickly and consistently outranks one who receives snaps without sending many back, even if total volume looks similar.

Weeks, not months

The system appears to weight recent weeks heavily, which is why a planet can shift noticeably after a single quiet or unusually active stretch.

How to check a planet

  1. Open a friend's profile

    Visit the profile of someone who has Snapchat+ and has the feature enabled.

  2. Look for a badge

    A Best Friends or Friends badge next to their name signals there's a solar system to see.

  3. Tap the badge

    Tapping reveals which planet you currently hold in their solar system, shown with a short animation.

Good to know: the planet you see on a friend's profile reflects your rank in their solar system, not theirs in yours. The two rankings are calculated separately, so they don't have to match.

Moving closer to the sun

Your position isn't fixed. A few habits shift it over time.

Snap more often

Frequent, regular snaps are one of the strongest signals behind the ranking, more so than a single burst of activity.

Keep the streak going

An unbroken streak reflects sustained contact, which tends to pull a friend inward toward Mercury over time.

Reply promptly

Two-way, timely conversation counts for more than messages that go unanswered for days.

None of this is a shortcut worth chasing on its own. The ranking is meant to reflect a friendship that's already active, not create one. Trying to manufacture a Mercury spot with forced daily snaps to someone you rarely otherwise talk to tends to feel hollow fast, for both people.

A quick note on how this feature has changed

Worth knowing if something here doesn't match what you're seeing.

Friend Solar System has been adjusted more than once since launch, including the shift from default-on to opt-in described earlier. Snap Inc. has also tested small tweaks to how quickly rankings update and how planets are revealed.

Because of that, small details on this page, like exact menu names or animation behaviour, can drift slightly out of date. The core mechanics, top eight friends ranked by activity, described with planets, have stayed consistent.

Planet order questions

Can I have fewer than eight planets?

Yes. If you have fewer than eight friends who meet the activity threshold, your solar system simply shows however many qualify.

Do planets ever repeat between friends?

No, within one person's solar system each planet is used once, since it represents a single rank position from one to eight.

Why did a friend suddenly jump several planets?

A sudden change usually follows a burst of activity, like a new streak or a stretch of daily snaps, or the opposite: a long gap in contact from another friend that shifted the ranking around them.

Does location or Snap Map affect the planet order?

No. The ranking is based on messaging and snap activity, not location sharing.
